# References and provenance

Accessed 13 November 2026. The literature scan was targeted and is not a systematic review.

1. **OECD (2026).** *Anti-Corruption and Integrity Outlook 2026: Harnessing the Integrity Advantage*, Chapter 10, “Integrity in public procurement.”  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/anti-corruption-and-integrity-outlook-2026_16708b78-en/full-report/component-14.html  
   Used for the public-procurement problem, lifecycle risks, prevalence of integrity indicators, and the warning that indicators require contextual investigation.

2. **OECD (2025).** *OECD Guidelines for Fighting Bid Rigging in Public Procurement (2025 Update)*, Chapter 3, “Bid-Rigging Detection List.”  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-guidelines-for-fighting-bid-rigging-in-public-procurement-2025-update_cbe05a56-en/full-report/component-5.html  
   Used for the distinction between suspicious indicators and proof, and the need to record and escalate suspicions through appropriate channels.

3. **Rystrøm, J., Schmitz, C., Korgul, K., Batzner, J., & Russell, C. (2026).** “Agent Benchmarks Fail Public Sector Requirements.” arXiv:2601.20617 (preprint; not treated as peer-reviewed).  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20617  
   Used to motivate process-based and public-sector-specific agent evaluation. The authors report an analysis of more than 1,300 benchmark papers and argue that no single benchmark met all their proposed criteria.

4. **TenderGate (2026).** *ProcureBench: LLM benchmark on procurement work*, benchmark page updated 11 September 2026.  
   https://tendergate.ai/benchmark  
   Vendor-hosted benchmark, not a peer-reviewed paper. It evaluates requirements extraction, bid analysis, and market research on a municipal school-renovation tender in English and Latvian. Included to make the overlap assessment explicit.

5. **Hendrata, F., Vanany, I., Suwignjo, P., & Siswanto, N. (2026).** “An Automating Tendering Performance Interpretation via Process Mining and an LLM-Based Agent.” *Engineering, Technology & Applied Science Research*, 16(2), 33031–33041.  
   https://doi.org/10.48084/etasr.16343  
   Adjacent work integrating process mining and an LLM agent to interpret tender performance outputs. Included in the overlap assessment.

6. **OECD (2025).** *Governing with Artificial Intelligence*, section “AI in public procurement.”  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_795de142-en/full-report/ai-in-public-procurement_2e095543.html  
   Used for AI opportunities, data-governance constraints, and procurement-cycle context.

7. **UK Cabinet Office (2025).** *PPN 017: Improving transparency of AI use in procurement*, published 17 February 2025.  
   https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ppn-017-improving-transparency-of-ai-use-in-procurement  
   Official UK policy note on transparency questions for AI use in procurement and government-service delivery.

8. **UK Cabinet Office (2026).** *Publishing contract data*, updated 29 January 2026.  
   https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/open-standards-for-government/open-contracting-data-standard-profile  
   Used to identify OCDS v1.1 as a common contracting-data model for a future real-data extension.

9. **Open Contracting Partnership.** *Open Contracting Data Standard.*  
   https://www.open-contracting.org/data-standard/  
   Describes the open standard and the linked planning, tender, award, contract, and implementation stages.

10. **Andersson, P. E., Arbin, K., & Rosenqvist, C. (2025).** “Assessing the value of artificial intelligence (AI) in governmental public procurement.” *Journal of Public Procurement*, 25(1), 120–139.  
    https://doi.org/10.1108/JOPP-05-2024-0057  
    Empirical study of perceived benefits and implementation challenges. Publisher page was not directly retrievable during this scan; bibliographic details and abstract were cross-checked against indexed metadata.

11. **Schmid, M., & Willems, J. (2026).** “AI for integrity: Predicting public servants’ susceptibility to corruption via supervised machine learning.” *Government Information Quarterly*, 43(3), 102158.  
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.giq.2026.102158  
    Recent adjacent work on predictive public-integrity analytics; it does not evaluate procurement process agents.

12. **López-Iturriaga, F. J., & Sanz, I. P. (2017).** “Predicting Public Corruption with Neural Networks: An Analysis of Spanish Provinces.” *Social Indicators Research*, 140, 975–998.  
    https://doi.org/10.1007/s11205-017-1802-2  
    Example of adjacent predictive risk analytics; not an agent-process benchmark.
